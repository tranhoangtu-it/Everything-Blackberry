# BlackBerry Historical Timeline — 1984 to 2026

> A comprehensive timeline of Research In Motion (RIM) and BlackBerry Limited, from founding to the present day.

---

## 🗓️ The 1980s — Origins

### 1984
- **November**: Mike Lazaridis and Douglas Fregin found **Research In Motion (RIM)** in Waterloo, Ontario, Canada.
- The company initially provides electronic display systems and data radio communications equipment.
- Mike Lazaridis is 23 years old at founding.

### 1987
- RIM develops wireless point-of-sale technology for gas stations and retail.
- Signs a contract with Ericsson for radio modems.

---

## 🗓️ The 1990s — Building the Foundation

### 1992
- Jim Balsillie joins as co-CEO, providing business leadership to complement Lazaridis's engineering vision.
- RIM wins a contract to develop a two-way radio modem for RAM Mobile Data (later Cingular).

### 1994
- RIM develops the **DigiSync Editor Interface Kit** — a system used in Hollywood film editing. Wins an Emmy award for this technology.

### 1996
- RIM releases the **RIM 900 Inter@ctive Pager** — the first two-way pager capable of wireless email.
- This device, though primitive, establishes the core concept of always-on mobile email.

### 1997
- RIM goes public on the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX: RIM).

### 1998
- **September**: Launch of the **RIM Inter@ctive Pager 950** — often considered the true ancestor of the BlackBerry. It uses the Mobitex network for data transmission.

### 1999
- **September**: Launch of the **BlackBerry 850** — the first device to officially carry the "BlackBerry" name.
- The name "BlackBerry" was chosen by Lexicon Branding: the keyboard resembled a blackberry drupe, and the word had a pleasing sound.
- The 850 adds proper email integration and a thumb keyboard.

---

## 🗓️ The 2000s — Enterprise Dominance

### 2000
- RIM launches the **BlackBerry Enterprise Server (BES)** — middleware that allows corporations to push email to BlackBerry devices in real time.
- BES becomes the secret weapon that locks BlackBerry into enterprise IT. Once a company runs BES, switching away is painful.

### 2001
- RIM launches **BlackBerry Internet Service (BIS)** for consumer email.
- The post-9/11 US Congressional response to the attacks is largely coordinated via BlackBerry devices. This moment cements BlackBerry's reputation for reliability and security.

### 2002
- Launch of the **BlackBerry 5810** — first BlackBerry with a phone (GSM). Required headphones as there was no speaker.

### 2003
- The **BlackBerry 7230** popularizes the device in enterprise globally.
- RIM signs its one-millionth BES seat.

### 2004
- Launch of the **BlackBerry 7100t** with the controversial SureType keyboard — two letters per key with predictive text.
- First consumer-targeted BlackBerry. Available at retail.

### 2005
- **BlackBerry** is named one of the fastest-growing brands by Interbrand.
- US politicians, executives, and celebrities are photographed with BlackBerries. The "CrackBerry" nickname — a play on crack cocaine addiction — enters the cultural lexicon.
- RIM acquires **Slango Inc.**, adding an additional engineering team.

### 2006
- Launch of **BlackBerry Pearl 8100** — first BB with a camera and the iconic optical trackball.
- RIM subscriber count passes **5 million**.

### 2007
- **January**: Apple announces the first iPhone at Macworld. Steve Jobs calls it "5 years ahead of anything out there."
  - Lazaridis reportedly dismisses the iPhone: *"The problem with those things is, they all have full browsers... that's going to break the network."*
- Launch of the **BlackBerry Curve 8300** — the best-selling consumer BB to date.
- RIM surpasses **10 million subscribers**.

### 2008
- Launch of the **BlackBerry Bold 9000** — the premium flagship. 3G, full QWERTY, premium build quality.
- Launch of the **BlackBerry Storm** — RIM's first all-touch phone with the "SurePress" clickable screen. Widely criticized for software bugs and sluggish performance.
- RIM surpasses **21 million subscribers**. Stock reaches **all-time high of USD $147.55**.

### 2009
- Android 1.0 (HTC G1) launches. The smartphone war escalates.
- Launch of BlackBerry **App World** — RIM's app store. Arrives 14 months after Apple's App Store.
- RIM surpasses **32 million subscribers**.
- Internal report warns executives that iPhone is "eating our lunch" in the consumer market. Executives reportedly dismiss the report.

---

## 🗓️ The 2010s — Rise, Fall, and Transformation

### 2010
- RIM's subscriber count peaks at **approximately 50 million** during the year.
- **April**: Apple launches iPad; Samsung Galaxy S launches.
- Launch of the **BlackBerry Torch 9800** — first BB with a touchscreen slider keyboard. Reviewed as too little, too late.
- Anonymous internal memo circulates titled "Innovation vs Execution". It warns of complacency.

### 2011
- **April**: Launch of the **BlackBerry PlayBook** tablet running **QNX** — a real-time operating system RIM acquired for USD $200 million.
  - The QNX acquisition would become the foundation for BB10.
  - The PlayBook launches without a native email client — requires "Bridge" from a BB phone. Widely mocked.
- Launch of the **BlackBerry Bold 9900/9930** — the last great BB7 phone. Full QWERTY + touchscreen.
- **August**: RIM shares drop 50% in one year. Analysts begin questioning the company's future.
- The "crackberry.com" community (founded 2004) reaches peak activity.

### 2012
- **January**: Mike Lazaridis steps down as CEO. Jim Balsillie also steps down.
- **January**: Thorsten Heins becomes sole CEO.
- **July**: RIM lays off 5,000 employees — 30% of its workforce.
- **September**: First BB10 developer preview event. The platform shows promise.
- RIM subscriber count begins declining.

### 2013
- **January**: At a major launch event, RIM rebrands to **BlackBerry** (company name changes from RIM to BlackBerry Limited).
- **January 30**: Launch of **BlackBerry 10** and the **Z10** (touchscreen) and **Q10** (QWERTY) simultaneously.
  - BB10 is universally praised by tech critics as innovative, beautiful, and genuinely different.
  - Sales are disappointing in the consumer market due to years of negative press and app ecosystem deficit.
- **November**: John Chen becomes CEO, replacing Thorsten Heins.
- BlackBerry signs a USD $1 billion credit facility. The company is in crisis.

### 2014
- John Chen begins executing his "software and services" pivot strategy.
- **September**: Launch of the **BlackBerry Passport** — the bold, square-screen device. Gets significant media attention.
- **December**: Launch of the **BlackBerry Classic** — restores the trackpad and navigation toolbelt, beloved by BB7 veterans.
- BB10 10.3 launches with full Android Runtime, allowing APK sideloading.

### 2015
- BlackBerry announces it will consider Android devices to reach larger market.
- **April**: Launch of **BlackBerry Leap** — budget touch BB10.
- **September**: Launch of **BlackBerry Passport Silver Edition**.
- **November**: Launch of the **BlackBerry Priv** — the first Android BlackBerry, with a QWERTY slider keyboard.

### 2016
- **January**: John Chen officially states BlackBerry is getting out of hardware.
- BlackBerry will license its brand and security software to hardware partners.
- **June**: Launch of **DTEK50** — first BlackBerry-branded Android by TCL.
- **September**: Launch of **DTEK60** by TCL.

### 2017
- **February**: Launch of **BlackBerry KEYone** (codename Mercury) by TCL — a physical keyboard Android phone. Sells out immediately. Demand far exceeds expectations.
- **April**: BlackBerry announces it is discontinuing all BB10 hardware development. BB10 OS will continue to receive security updates through 2020.
- **June**: The **BlackBerry Classic** is officially discontinued.
- TCL and other partners continue making BlackBerry-branded Android phones.

### 2018
- **June**: Launch of **BlackBerry KEY2** by TCL. Best physical keyboard Android phone ever made.
- **September**: Launch of **BlackBerry KEY2 LE** — budget variant of KEY2.
- BlackBerry Limited (the software company) focuses on:
  - **QNX** (embedded OS, used in millions of cars)
  - **CylancePROTECT** (AI-based endpoint security, acquired 2019)
  - **BlackBerry Spark** enterprise security platform

### 2019
- **May 31**: **BBM (BlackBerry Messenger) shuts down** permanently.
- BlackBerry acquires **Cylance** for USD $1.4 billion.
- BlackBerry's market cap is now based on its software business, not hardware.

---

## 🗓️ The 2020s — Legacy and Revival Attempts

### 2020
- BlackBerry announces it will allow TCL's license to expire.
- **TCL announces** (August) it will stop making BlackBerry-branded phones after August 31, 2020. The KEY2 is the last TCL BlackBerry.

### 2021
- **OnwardMobility**, a startup, announces a new 5G BlackBerry keyboard Android phone in partnership with FIH Mobile.
- Nostalgia and demand for physical keyboard phones spikes.
- **January 4, 2022** is announced as the shutdown date for all BlackBerry infrastructure (BIS/BES).

### 2022
- **January 4**: BlackBerry's device services permanently shut down.
  - BIS (BlackBerry Internet Service) offline
  - BES (BlackBerry Enterprise Server) cloud services offline
  - BlackBerry Messenger (already shut down in 2019)
  - BlackBerry World app store closes
  - BlackBerry Protect device tracking offline
  - BBID authentication servers offline
  - **All BB10 and BB7 devices affected**
- **January**: OnwardMobility ceases operations without releasing the promised new BlackBerry. Customers are disappointed.
- The BB10 community adapts: bypass guides, certificate fixes, and sideloading become essential knowledge.
- CrackBerry forums experience a spike in traffic as users troubleshoot their devices.

### 2023
- BlackBerry Limited (software company) splits:
  - **IoT/QNX division** retained
  - **Cybersecurity division** (Cylance) sold to Arctic Wolf Networks for USD $160 million — considered a significant loss compared to the purchase price.
- QNX powers 235+ million vehicles. BlackBerry QNX is embedded in car infotainment, ADAS, and medical devices.

### 2024
- Growing "digital detox" movement drives renewed interest in BlackBerry devices.
- Used BlackBerry Classic and Passport prices increase on eBay and secondhand markets.
- The Everything-BlackBerry GitHub project and similar community archives launch.

### 2025
- Community continues to develop and maintain BB10 tools:
  - Sachesi 2.x maintains BB10 firmware download capability
  - Bgram (Telegram client for BB10) active development continues
  - Certificate fix guides widely shared
- BlackBerry KEY2 units with Android 10 remain in daily use by thousands.

### 2026 (Current Year)
- **BlackBerry Limited** (the software company) focuses entirely on QNX and enterprise cybersecurity.
- **No new BlackBerry-branded smartphones** exist or are planned.
- **BB10 hardware**: All devices fully functional offline and on Wi-Fi. LTE data on supported carriers still works.
- The enthusiast community on Telegram and CrackBerry remains active.
- This repository serves as the definitive technical reference.
